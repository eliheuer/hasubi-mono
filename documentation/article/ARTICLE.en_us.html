Hasubi Mono is a condensed monospace designed by Eli Heuer for working with Arabic text in terminals and code editors. Its name comes from the Arabic حاسوبي, meaning “my computer.” The Arabic letterforms give the typeface its horizontal emphasis. The Latin letters share that emphasis, so a line of code can move between words and scripts while keeping a narrow column. To contribute, visit github.com/eliheuer/hasubi-mono.

The gaps between joining Arabic letters draw on an example of metal type in Materials for the Study of the Bábí Religion, published in 1918. While learning Arabic, Heuer found that these breaks helped him distinguish the letters in a word. In Hasubi Mono the gaps sit within the rhythm of connected writing.

The variable font runs from Regular to Black. A print specimen pairs the Arabic family name, حاسوبي مونو, with a short Python program beginning def grid():. These settings show the same letters in two working contexts, reading Arabic and editing code. Hebrew belongs to the design brief and is still in development.
